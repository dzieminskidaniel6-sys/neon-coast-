# NEON COAST — Character Arcs & Dialogue Reference

## Alex
Starts as an outsider focused only on finding Leo. Across the campaign he learns that the truth affects people who had no part in the disappearance. Player decisions define whether he becomes a protector, an uncompromising whistleblower, or someone willing to bargain. Do not assign a fixed personality in dialogue: offer calm, blunt and humorous responses.

## Leo
Absent during the opening chapters but present through repair records, messages and memories. Reveal that he found discrepancies in Harbor Authority manifests and tried to copy proof. His messages should gradually move from practical clues to personal apologies. Final reunion must acknowledge that Alex may have made choices Leo disagrees with.

## Maya
Public face: confident, funny under pressure, practical. Private conflict: she knew Leo was investigating and withheld one detail because she feared the port would retaliate against her family. Arc: from cautious guide to a trusted partner, estranged contact or late-game rescuer depending on Maya trust and how Alex handles evidence.

## Nico
Public face: mechanic with dry humour and a talent for improvising. Private conflict: he altered one of Leo's repair records after being threatened. Arc: admit the alteration, help expose the manifest or retreat to protect his garage. High trust unlocks a vehicle preparation option in mission 16.

## Vesper
Public face: information broker who treats every conversation as negotiation. Private conflict: she once sold a shipment schedule without knowing Leo was connected to it. Arc: she can become a reliable information ally, a strictly transactional contact or an opportunist who sells the player's plan. Respect agreements and settle debts to unlock her support.

## Adrian Vale
Proposed antagonist, pending final canon approval. Public face: polished Harbor Authority executive and civic benefactor. Private logic: he believes the port's stability justifies secrecy and coercion. He should be persuasive, not cartoonishly evil. The player should understand his argument without being required to agree.

## Dialogue rules
- Keep spoken lines short enough to read during gameplay.
- Give each character a distinct rhythm and vocabulary.
- Avoid explaining facts the player already discovered.
- Use optional lines for backstory, but keep critical clues in mandatory beats.
- Every choice must have an authored response and a recorded consequence.
- Avoid fake choices that always lead to identical reactions.
- Allow a neutral or de-escalating option in tense scenes when practical.

## Ending evaluation draft
- The City Knows: evidence_strength >= 3 AND public_support >= 2.
- Blood Before Truth: leo_bond >= 3 AND player chooses rescue-first.
- A Deal in the Dark: evidence_strength >= 2 AND vesper_debt <= 1 AND player accepts Vale's offer.
- No One Gets Away: evidence_strength >= 3 AND at least two major ally relationships <= -1.
- The Signal Goes Silent: none of the above; present as a failure ending, not a random punishment.

These thresholds are balancing placeholders. The final game should explain the decisive conditions through journal clues and character dialogue, not expose hidden numbers.
